Product law

Why there is no timeline

The competitor is not Premiere. It's "I'll do it later."

Premiere's price is $23 a month. Its cost is 40 GB of disk, a media cache that grows until you notice, a proxy workflow, a six-minute launch, and a render queue you have to babysit. On a laptop with 256 GB and 8 GB of RAM, that cost is the entire reason the clip never gets made. So the thing to attack is not the feature list — it's the ritual. A URL, a paste, ninety seconds.

A timeline is a skeuomorph of a physical strip of film.

It's the right interface for a wedding video and the wrong one for a podcast, where every edit you actually want is a sentence-level decision. Eight hours of footage contains maybe forty decisions worth making — is this funny, yes or no — and a timeline makes you scrub 12 GB of pixels to find them. Text is the interface everyone already knows. Strike the sentence; the video follows.

You never needed the pixels.

This is the whole engineering trick. To find a clip inside 8 hours you need the transcript (54,000 words with per-word timings ≈ 2.3 MB) and the loudness envelope (10 Hz, 295,000 samples ≈ 1.1 MB). That is a 3.4 MB search index for 12.4 GB of video — three hundredths of one percent of it, and it fits in the RAM of a phone. Scoring every 17–58 s window in all 8 hours takes about 250 ms. Pixels only get decoded for the 45 seconds you chose, at the moment you render, one frame at a time. Peak memory is one frame. Peak disk is the finished short.

Premiere assumes random access to decoded pixels. Sieve assumes you're looking for a sentence.

The engine end of it is measured, not asserted: ffprobe reads 1.75 MB (the header, then a ranged seek for the moov atom at the end of the file), ffmpeg reads the file through exactly once with the video track never decoded, and 0 bytes are written to disk. That's 1.30× the file size in total, and it hands back 0.1% of it. cd sieve-engine && npm run check re-runs that measurement against a file whose moov atom is deliberately at the end — the case that breaks every "just pipe it into stdin" design.

The sounds are oscillators, not samples.

Every meme SFX in here is synthesised at render time out of two or three oscillators and a filter. That's zero MB of sound library, zero licensing, and — the part that actually matters — nothing for YouTube's Content ID to match against, because a vine boom made of a sine sweep is not a recording of anything. A downloaded meme pack is a copyright claim waiting to happen.

The ranking argues its case.

Auto-clippers fail in one specific way: they hand you a clip that opens on "so that's exactly why he did it" — a pronoun with no antecedent, and the viewer is gone in 1.2 seconds. So Self-contained is a first-class signal here, alongside Hook, Peak, Curiosity, Closure and Pace. Six bars, each legible, each overridable, and an honest disclaimer: this ranks candidates, it doesn't predict views. Nothing predicts views. What it does is turn 8 hours into 24 decisions.

What Sieve refuses to be

No timeline. No media bins. No project files. No account. No upload of your footage to us — the engine streams from your Drive and keeps nothing. No AI voice, no AI avatar, no auto-generated slop: every word in the output is a word somebody actually said. No "smart" edit you can't see and can't undo.
